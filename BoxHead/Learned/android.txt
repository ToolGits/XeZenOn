Android

Android is an open-source software stack designed for a wide range of devices, including smartphones, tablets, TVs, cars, watches, and other devices.

Android was developed by Android Inc. and later acquired by Google.

In 2007, Google and the Open Handset Alliance announced Android as an open mobile platform.

Android 1.0 was released in 2008 with the first commercial Android device, the T-Mobile G1.

Android 1.5 was called Cupcake and introduced features such as virtual keyboards and home screen widgets.

Android 1.6 was called Donut and improved search, screen support, and device compatibility.

Android 2.0 and 2.1 were called Eclair and introduced several improvements to the interface and device features.

Android 2.2 was called Froyo and improved performance and system functionality.

Android 2.3 was called Gingerbread and introduced improvements to performance, power management, and hardware support.

Android 3.0 was called Honeycomb and was designed mainly for tablets.

Android 4.0 was called Ice Cream Sandwich and unified the phone and tablet versions of Android.

Android 4.1 was called Jelly Bean and improved performance, notifications, and the user experience.

Android 4.4 was called KitKat and focused on improving performance and making Android available to more devices.

Android 5.0 was called Lollipop and introduced Material Design.

Android 6.0 was called Marshmallow and introduced runtime permissions and improved power management.

Android 7.0 was called Nougat and introduced features such as split-screen multitasking and improved notifications.

Android 8.0 was called Oreo and introduced features such as picture-in-picture and Android Go.

Android 9 was called Pie and introduced gesture navigation and Digital Wellbeing features.

Android 10 removed the dessert name from the public version name and introduced features such as system-wide dark mode and additional privacy controls.

Android 11 improved privacy, notifications, device controls, and media controls.

Android 12 introduced Material You, a redesigned and more customizable interface.

Android 13 improved privacy, notifications, per-app languages, and support for large screens.

Android 14 introduced further improvements to security, accessibility, battery life, and system customization.

Android 15 continued improving privacy, security, large-screen support, and system features.

Android continues to evolve as an open-source platform with support for many types of devices and hardware.

Android applications can be developed using technologies such as Kotlin, Java, and the Android SDK.

The Android Open Source Project provides the open-source foundation of Android.
